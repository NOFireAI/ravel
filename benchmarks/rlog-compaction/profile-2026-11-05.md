### Realism verdict

Every 230-input run is inside the 8,000-70,000 rec/s/core band. The range
runs from 13,169 (the profiled run, which the sampler slows) to 18,794
(zstd 3). The bin's `checks.realism` is `pass` and `checks.failures` is
empty on all seven runs. The band is not applied to the 60-input run
(`not applied: the band is calibrated at --inputs 230, this run has 60`), and
that run's `failures` is empty. Measured 2026-11-06.

### Page-choice A/B (measured 2026-11-06)

The 9 published L1 parts at zstd level 9 were decoded and re-encoded. The two
choices ran on two threads at the same time, and each figure is that
thread's CPU clock.

| Page choice | Bytes | L1/L0 | Encode thread CPU s | Decode thread CPU s | Parts byte-identical to published |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exhaustive (compactor default) | 364,576,588 | 0.9278 | 147.4 | 12.1 | 9 of 9 |
| Estimated | 366,672,064 | 0.9332 | 104.2 | 12.6 | 0 of 9 |

Estimated costs 29% less encode CPU (43.2 s less) for 0.57% more bytes
(2,095,476). Exhaustive reproduces every published part byte for byte, which
confirms the re-encode uses the compactor's writer settings. Measured
2026-11-06.

## Reading

- One merge of a 230-input, 2,477-stream hour runs on one thread for its
  whole length. The time goes to building the L1 parts, not to reading the
  inputs. All 230 inputs are fetched in resident mode, one GET each, in
  under a millisecond against memory.
- The part writer's own column work (`BlockEncoder::encode`, 50.4%
  inclusive) costs more than its zstd (32.6%). Lowering the zstd level from
  9 to 3 cut process CPU from a median 185.0 s to 145.8 s, for 1.9% more
  bytes (371,333,324 against 364,576,588).
- `PageChoice::Estimated` cuts 29% of encode CPU for 0.57% more bytes.
