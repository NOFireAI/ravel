- **A logs flush above the write gate's inline floor seals its column pages in
  parallel on the gate** (issue #3499, ADR-3492 decision 7). Each block's page
  jobs go into a shared queue that the flush seals from under its own permit,
  while page tasks on the blocking pool that get a write-gate permit take
  page jobs from the same queue; a page task that finds the queue empty
  releases its permit without work. `ravel_cpu_gate_jobs_total{gate="write",site="log_flush"}`
  counts the flush's job plus one per page job a page task sealed. With no
  free permit, or a block of one job, the flush seals its pages serially
  under its own permit and counts one job; below the floor it stays one
  inline job. Stored objects are byte-identical to the serial encode, strict
  acks still wait for the data object and its commit record, and a failed
  page job fails the flush with the retryable `ShardUnavailable` and counts
  on `flush_gate_faults`. Measured on the `logseg_encode` bench (#3508), the
  7.5k-record flush goes from 181.7 ms to 169.9 ms on the bench's 4-thread
  pool; ADR-3492's D7 measurement amendment records the revised strict-ack
  prediction and why the gated encode may do less well.
