- A SQL statement refused for memory now retries through a hash exchange and the bucketed final aggregate when it reads exactly one `logs` scan, its final aggregate's group keys and state columns are all fixed-width and exactly typed, and its plan holds only operators the retry knows how to spill; every other statement, and any whose derived plan would not fit the open-file budget, keeps the sort-merge retry. The bucket count and chunk size are derived from the process's open-file limit and from a memory limit, and the bucketed retry's memory pool runs at that same limit: the per-query limit when the per-query limit refused the first attempt, or, when the tenant or process budget refused it, the room that budget had left for this statement at the refusal (its limit less what other statements, and on the process budget the fetch layer and caches, held), capped at the per-query limit. The retry's log line names `retry_plan` (`bucket`, `bucket_without_disk` when the bucketed plan ran with no disk manager that allows temp files, or `sort_merge`), the reason a statement kept the sort-merge retry, the derived `bits` and `routed_chunk_rows`, the limit the retry was sized by (`effective_limit_bytes`), and `retry_refusal`, the refusal that ended a failed retry, read from its error (`spill_hard_bound` and `spill_cap` name the spill-mode pool's own bounds). Startup logs two more lines, `sql_spill_retry_descriptor_budget` (the soft open-file limit over twice the spill share count) and `sql_spill_retry_modes`, which names the bucketed retry only when that budget is at least three descriptors per exchange partition.
