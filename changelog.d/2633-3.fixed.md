- **The SQL memory pool keeps a grouped aggregate's released bytes charged
  until its stream ends** (issue #2633). DataFusion shrinks a grouped hash
  aggregate's reservation when it emits, while the emitted batch still holds
  those bytes, and the pool used to hand them to other queries at once. The
  pool now holds them on the query, tenant and process budgets until the
  aggregate's stream is dropped. The hold is chosen per stream: it applies to
  an aggregate DataFusion marks as unable to spill, and never to a partial
  aggregate or to a final aggregate that can spill to disk. While a downstream
  operator, for example a Sort, reserves the emitted batches it is handed, the
  same bytes are charged twice, so a statement that used to fit under
  `--sql-max-query-bytes` can now be refused with 422 `ResourcesExhausted`. The
  extra charge is bounded by the aggregate's own peak reservation. The sizing
  guidance for `--sql-max-query-bytes` is unchanged: a measured `GROUP BY`
  matrix showed no peak change beyond run-to-run spread, though a statement
  that reserves the aggregate's output downstream was not measured.
